Folder numbering
================

The numbers follow the ten-problem one-pager, not the whitepaper's pitfall
sequence. Folder 07 is the exercise behind problem 7 on that page, and so on
through 10. This replaces the earlier scheme, which ordered by whitepaper
pitfall and covered eleven exercises.

Two exercises are not on the one-pager. They sit at 11 and 12 so they sort
last and the gap is visible without needing a footnote.

Why 11 and 12 are not on the one-pager
--------------------------------------

Both were merged into neighbouring problems when the page was cut from twenty
pitfalls to ten, on the grounds that a client hears them as one complaint
rather than two.

  11  Chunking damage folded into problem 2. To a buyer, "it cuts my tables
      in half" and "it cannot find part numbers" are the same complaint:
      it cannot find the right thing. Precision is the stronger of the pair
      to lead with, because part numbers and negation are a failure the
      buyer has already experienced.

  12  Memory-priced vector serving folded into problem 7. To a CFO, the
      token bill and the index bill are one line item. The whitepaper also
      treats this pitfall as the furthest along the maturity arc, with
      nothing fundamental left unsolved, which makes it a weak thing to
      lead with.

Both exercises still stand on their own and are worth keeping. They are
supporting evidence rather than headline material.

Whitepaper pitfalls each exercise leads with
--------------------------------------------

Kept for reference, since the whitepaper numbering has not changed:

  01  pitfall 1   stale indexes and rebuild-by-replay
  02  pitfall 3   similarity is not relevance (hybrid retrieval, reranking)
  03  pitfall 4   no corpus-level answers (graph retrieval)
  04  pitfall 5   grounded hallucination (faithfulness evaluation)
  05  pitfall 7   permission and personal data in the index (erasure by replay)
  06  pitfall 8   injection via the corpus
  07  pitfalls 11, 13, 16  token spend, provider dependency, capability tiers
  08  pitfall 17  agent error and cost compounding (bounded autonomy)
  09  pitfall 18  tool sprawl and the MCP attack surface
  10  pitfalls 14, 19  non-determinism, and who did what (audit and attribution)
  11  pitfall 2   chunking destroys meaning
  12  pitfall 10  memory-priced vector infrastructure (disk-first ANN)

Six of the twenty pitfalls have no exercise: 6 (time-blind indexes),
9 (embedder lock-in), 12 (model churn), 15 (long context is not solved
context), and 20 (the compliance burden), which is discharged by 05 and 10
rather than by an exercise of its own.
