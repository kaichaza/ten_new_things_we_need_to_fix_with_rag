The Fruit Shop was founded twelve years ago by Ingrid's aunt, who ran a market
stall before taking over the corner premises. From the first day the shop has
sold exactly three fruits: apples, bananas and pears. The founding rule,
painted above the till, reads "three fruits, done well".

Prices are kept deliberately simple so that children can do the arithmetic:
apples cost 3 per kilogram, bananas cost 2 per kilogram, and pears cost 4 per
kilogram. There is no discount tier and no loyalty pricing; what is on the
sign is what everyone pays, including regulars.

The grape question comes up every week. The shop does not sell grapes and
never has. The stated reason is that the local grape supply is unreliable in
quality, and the owner refuses to sell fruit she would not eat herself. Staff
are trained to suggest pears to anyone asking for grapes.

Three regular customers anchor the week. Ingrid buys apples every Monday for
baking. Kwon buys bananas in bulk for a running club. Tendai prefers pears
and once bought the entire crate before a family gathering. The current
customer, Matt, is newer and mostly asks questions before buying anything.

Deliveries arrive on Tuesdays and Fridays before opening. Anything bruised is
set aside and sold at half price from a basket by the door, which is the only
exception to the fixed-price rule and the closest thing the shop has to a
promotion.
